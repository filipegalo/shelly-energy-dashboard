# Solar PV Feasibility Report: Alhandra / São João dos Montes

**Date:** 23/10/2026
**Data:** Shelly energy export, 1–30 September 2026 (one month only)
**Location assumed:** Alhandra / São João dos Montes, Vila Franca de Xira (38.94 N, −9.03 E)

> **How to read this report.** September figures are **measured**. Annual figures are **modelled scenarios** extrapolated from one month of consumption data. Costs and export prices are **market indications, not quotes**.

---

## 1. Recommendation

**Install about 4 kWp, split between the south and west roof sections, without a battery for now, and set the EV to charge from solar.**

- **Payback:** about 11.4 years at mid-range 2026 prices, saving about €570/yr. If at least half of each day's EV charging moves into daylight, payback drops to about 9 years and savings rise to about €710/yr.
- **Why not 3 kWp:** it has the shortest payback (10.8 years) but saves less in total. The 4th kWp still pays back in about 14 years on its own, well within a 25-year panel life.
- **Why not 5 kWp or more:** each extra kWp mostly adds unpaid or low-paid export. On its own, the 5th kWp pays back in about 17 years, the 6th in about 19 and the 7th–8th in about 22. A 5 kWp array is only worth it if the car is reliably at home and charging during the day.
- **Keep the inverter at 4 kW or below.** Above 4 kW, Portuguese rules require remote metering of all production at the owner's cost. A 4 kW inverter loses almost nothing even with 5 kWp of panels, because the south and west sections peak at different times. Confirm with installers how "installed capacity" is counted.
- **Batteries are not justified at current prices.** Payback on the extra cost is about 15–21 years for 5 kWh and 15–29 years for 10 kWh, longer than a typical 10-year battery warranty.
- **These are scenarios, not measurements.** The ranking of the options is reasonably robust; the exact euro amounts are not.

### Data quality

- No duplicate records.
- 12 collection outages totalling about 49 hours (7% of the month). The longest were 15 h on 9–10 Sep and about 9 h each on 6 and 30 Sep.
- Only 16 of the 30 days are at least 90% complete.

- Source: PVGIS, roof-mounted, 2014–2023 hourly weather data, with PVGIS's standard 14% system loss (covers the inverter, cabling and dirt on the panels) plus temperature losses.
- Assumed tilt is 25° for both sections. This is an assumption; check the actual roof pitch.
- Yield per kWp per year: south 1,502 kWh, west 1,270 kWh. Year-to-year variation is about ±3%.
- Base case is a 50/50 south/west split. **Shading is unknown** and is not modelled beyond PVGIS's terrain horizon.

- Each year is simulated in 15-minute steps.

## 4. Batteries (modelled)

### Assumptions

- 90% of nominal capacity is usable; round-trip efficiency is 90%.
- Power limits: 2.5 kW for the 5 kWh battery, 5 kW for the 10 kWh battery.
- The battery charges only from solar surplus.

**Extra cost (illustrative, from 4gnews):** 5 kWh about €3,200 (range €2,000–4,200); 10 kWh about €5,500 (range €4,000–6,400).

### Results

| Added to 4 kWp | Extra solar used at home, kWh/yr | Export reduction, kWh | Extra savings | Payback on extra cost: base (range) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 kWh | 1,351 | 1,501 | €185/yr | **17 yrs** (11–23) |
| 10 kWh | 2,055 | 2,283 | €281/yr | **20 yrs** (14–23) |

- On larger systems the battery paybacks are slightly better: 15–16 years at 5–8 kWp. Only the lowest cost estimates get close to 10 years.
- **Each stored kWh only saves about €0.14 net:** it avoids roughly €0.17 of grid electricity but gives up €0.03 of export income.
- **Moving EV charging to daytime does part of the battery's job for free.** Once 50% of EV charging is shifted, a 5 kWh battery adds only about €145/yr on a 4 kWp system.

## 5. Loads that can move into solar hours

- **EV charging (the only big one).**
  - Moving 50% of daily EV charging to the same day's sunny hours adds about €140/yr at 4 kWp. Moving 100% (an upper limit) adds about €215/yr.
  - This only works if the car is at home during the day. Office AC is 79% daytime, which suggests someone is often home, but the data can't confirm the car is.
  - The charger relay simply switches a roughly 2.3 kW load on and off. That works for "charge when there's a solar surplus" automation, but it can't follow the surplus smoothly.
- **Washing machines.** The night share is about 16 kWh/month, worth at most about €30/yr if moved to daytime.
- **Office AC.** Already mostly daytime, and summer cooling naturally coincides with solar.
- **Not shiftable:** the server/network, the fridge and the oven.
- **Hot water and heating: not identifiable.** No device measures them. An electric water heater, if there is one, is probably in the 164 kWh not covered by any device and could be a good candidate for moving to daytime.

## 6. Financial comparison

### Tariff assumptions

- Tariff as configured in the Shelly app: €0.137378/kWh before VAT, plus €0.001/kWh excise tax, with 6% VAT on the first 200 kWh per 30 days and 23% on the rest.
- Each kWh no longer imported is charged at 23% VAT, so it is worth about **€0.170**.
- Fixed charges (contracted power, audiovisual contribution, DGEG fee) are excluded from the bill figures because they stay the same after installation.

### Bill and payback, no battery

- Annual variable bill before solar: **about €1,513**.
- Installed prices include 23% VAT. The 6% rate on solar equipment ended on 30 June 2025, and no source was found showing the 2026 budget restored it.

### What the payback figures leave out

- Panel degradation (about 0.5%/yr).
- Electricity price inflation.
- Replacing the inverter around year 10–15.
- Any extra metering cost above 4 kW.

### Getting paid for export

- Export is only paid with a buyer contract. That also means opening activity with the tax authority (Finanças), although sales income up to €1,000/yr is IRS-exempt.
- Published prices are roughly €0.035–0.039/kWh fixed, or market-indexed. Without a contract, exported energy earns nothing.
- Because of this, the low export price weakens the case for larger systems.

### Check VAT eligibility

The 6% VAT on the first 200 kWh requires contracted power of 6.9 kVA or less, and the 8.9 kW peak suggests it may be higher. This changes the bill before solar, but not the savings, because each avoided kWh is charged at 23% either way.

## 7. Uncertainty

- **Annual consumption is extrapolated (about 9,230 kWh/yr) from one month.** September has no heating and little cooling.
  - Winter heating (electric towel rail, fireplace, AC used as a heat pump) and summer AC are unknown.
  - EV mileage may change with the seasons.
- **Seasonal timing is a bigger risk than total kWh.** If winter use is mostly evening heating, self-consumption falls just when solar output is lowest. That favours staying at 4 kWp or less, not going bigger.
- **About 8% of September's timing is reconstructed.** Days 6, 10 and 30 are the least reliable.
- **The 15-minute steps overstate self-consumption by a few percent.** Short spikes like the oven or kettle can't be matched exactly. Solar output is also spread evenly within each hour.
- **Roof tilt, shading and usable area are unknown.** Shading could cut generation substantially.
- **Costs and export prices are market indications, not quotes.** No source states a 2026 export price.

## 8. Next steps

### Data

1. **Get 12+ months of E-REDES smart-meter readings** (15-minute data, from the E-REDES Balcão Digital website). This is the single most valuable addition.
2. **Export all Shelly hourly history** (kept for 730 days), and **raise the Shelly minute-data retention to at least 400 days** now, before it ages out.
3. **Electricity bill:** contracted power, tariff type, and whether a two-period day/night tariff would be cheaper for night EV charging. A cheaper night rate would also reduce the value of batteries and of moving EV charging.

### The home

4. **Car availability and charger:** how many weekdays the car is at home in daylight, and whether a charger that follows the solar surplus is worth adding.
5. **Hot water and heating:** what the water heater and heating run on, and their consumption.

### Installers and buyers

6. **Installer site survey:** roof area per section, tilt, exact orientation and shading.
7. **Three written quotes for about 4 kWp.** Ask each to say whether the inverter stays at 4 kW or below, the per-kWp price for 5 kWp, and the battery price as a separate line.
8. **Compare surplus-buyer offers** using the ERSE comparison tool, before deciding whether exporting is worth registering for.
